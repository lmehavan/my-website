Grounds, not finished compost. The free bags contain used coffee grounds. They are an ingredient for composting, not ready-to-use compost or a complete potting mix.
01 / Make the connection

A good question starts it.

Starbucks' Grounds for Your Garden program gives used grounds another use. Ask at a participating store; availability and collection arrangements vary. This project does not reserve bags or confirm stock at any location.

01 / ASK

Check with a barista.

“Do you have any used coffee grounds available through Grounds for Your Garden?” No grounds today? Try another time or ask a local café about reuse.

02 / COLLECT

Take only what you can use.

Use staff-provided bags. Never retrieve grounds from bins. Carry damp grounds upright inside a container that catches leaks.

03 / PUT TO USE

Have a compost plan.

Add the grounds promptly to your compost mixture. No compost space? Ask a community garden whether it accepts grounds before taking them there.

02 / Mix, don't mound

A little coffee. Plenty of balance.

Washington State University Extension recommends keeping coffee grounds to no more than 20% of your compost pile by volume. Mix them with other ingredients, including dry leaves or shredded plain cardboard, and let everything break down.

A simple volume guide

1 part coffee grounds + at least 4 parts other compost ingredients.

This is a coffee-content limit, not a complete compost recipe. Your pile still needs varied materials, moisture and air.

Do

  • Mix grounds with varied compost materials.
  • Remove plastic packaging before composting.
  • Use the finished compost after decomposition.

Avoid

  • Using raw grounds alone as potting soil.
  • Leaving thick piles around plants.
  • Dumping grounds in park beds or tree pits.
03 / Before you go

A few useful answers.

Does every Starbucks have free bags?

Do not assume that every store participates or has bags ready. Ask staff about availability. Bag colors and packaging may vary; ask for used coffee grounds rather than a “green bag.”

Can I use the grounds directly in a houseplant pot?

For a simple beginner approach, compost them first with other materials. Used grounds alone are not a balanced growing medium, and excessive amounts can harm plants.

Are these grounds food or fresh coffee?

No. They have already been brewed and are being offered for reuse in composting or gardening, not for eating or brewing.

What if I don't have a garden?

You can still learn and share this guide. If you want to donate grounds, first confirm that a garden or composting group accepts them and follow its instructions. Avoid collecting bags without a destination.

Is this website run by Starbucks or ASU?

No. It is an independent student sustainability project by Luka Mzhavanadze, created for community education. It is not sponsored, endorsed or operated by Starbucks or Arizona State University.
